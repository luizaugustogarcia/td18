\begin{abstract}
The active route uses two transpositions producing three fixed symbols, followed
by a transposition producing at least two more, on either endpoint of a
cyclic-target pair. Domains have 19 symbols and root budget 10.
Unselected roots are generated directly. The endpoint cover consists of all
74 fixed-point-free even annotated types on 14 symbols, with budget
7. The checker independently regenerates roots, endpoints, and all inverse
waves and replays intrinsic sorting certificates. This document is a conditional
proof program; it does not establish $\TD(18)=10$ without the complete
checked artifacts in \nolinkurl{docs/RESEARCH_PLAN.md}.
\end{abstract}
\section{Pairs, moves, fixed symbols, and premises}
Let $N=n+1$. Let a cyclic-target pair be $(\omega,\beta)$ with even algebraic
permutation $\omega$, current $N$-cycle $\beta$, and target $\rho=\omega\beta$
also an $N$-cycle. Composition is from right to left. A transposition is an
applicable $3$-cycle $\tau$ ordered around the current cycle, acting as
$(\omega,\beta)\mapsto(\omega\tau^{-1},\tau\beta)$. Reserve $2$-cycle for an
algebraic cycle of length two. The gain counts newly fixed symbols. Existing
fixed points are first-class data and are contracted from both cycles before
using fixed-point-free generation or move tests. Contraction preserves distance
by the shared framework~\cite{framework},
\nolinkurl{prop:contraction-preserves-distance}.

A cycle on support $S$ is unoriented when it is the inverse of the current
cycle restricted to $S$; otherwise it is oriented. Define the $3$-norm by
$\kappa=(N-c_{\rm odd}(\omega))/2$, where $c_{\rm odd}$ includes fixed points.
A qualifying $2$-move increases that count by two and creates a fixed point.
The active rule does not require every first move to be qualifying and permits
first gain zero. It therefore imposes no unjustified $\kappa\leq6$ restriction
on the new endpoint inventory.

The separately certified shared-framework result $\TD(16)=9$ and the established smaller diameters are premises.
A known-diameter closure at a contracted domain of $m$ symbols is valid with
remaining budget $b$ exactly when $\TD(m-1)\leq b$ (distance zero for $m\leq1$).
A root with an existing fixed point closes using the established smaller bound.
The reverse root is separately closed by Eriksson et al.'s formula
$\lceil 19/2\rceil=10$~\cite{eriksson}.

\section{Joint selection on both endpoints}
Sorting distance on cycles is symmetric. Moving either endpoint by one edge
of its undirected transposition graph costs one in a triangle-inequality upper
bound. Interchanging endpoints is represented by $(\omega^{-1},\rho)$ and
costs nothing. This operation is used inside the argument; it is not silently
added to extended-toric class equivalence.

Lemma 5.1 of Eriksson et al.~\cite{eriksson} gives three bonds using two block
moves for a non-reverse permutation, permitting moves on either side. The
lemma is not asserted to be a literal two-move forward prefix preserving the
original target. Bonds correspond to algebraic fixed points. Test all choices,
not only one construction promised by the lemma:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Test immediate gains at least two on either endpoint. Contraction leaves
at most 17 symbols and $1+\TD(16)\leq 10$ closes the root.
\item Otherwise inspect every applicable first transposition, of gain zero or
one, on either endpoint. A second move must then gain at least three or at least
two respectively to produce three fixed symbols. Two moves producing at least
four fixed symbols close by $2+\TD(14)\leq 10$.
\item For every result with exactly three fixed symbols, contract them and test
every third move of gain at least two, on either endpoint. Gain three leaves at
most 13 symbols, closing by $3+\TD(12)\leq 10$. Gain two
reaches a 14-symbol obligation with 7 moves remaining.
\item Retain every root without any such route. Only the explicitly cited,
budget-valid support patterns below may clear oriented retained roots. Every
other retained root requires a result with budget 10.
\end{enumerate}

Every transposition creating at least two fixed points is supported, up to
cyclic writing, on $(x,\omega(x),\omega^2(x))$, in current cyclic order. Indeed,
its three changed images can fix two symbols only if two consecutive edges
agree with $\omega$; the converse follows by multiplying the permutations.
This characterization covers even-length cycles and across-cycle first supports.
Destroying one old fixed point permits at most two newly fixed symbols and net
gain at most one. Thus the required second and third moves preserve previously
fixed symbols; contracting them loses no eligible support. Totally unoriented
roots, including all-odd roots, still enter gain-zero first-move tests.

\section{Endpoint cover and direct root inventory}
The endpoint cover consists of every even partition of 14 into parts at
least two and every orientation annotation, with algebraic $2$-cycles always
unoriented. A length-$k$ multiplicity $m_k$ contributes $m_k+1$ choices for
$k\geq3$. The complete annotation count is 74. The precondition is fixed-point
freedom, after contracting all five created fixed symbols. Every class must
have a 7-move witness or valid diameter closure, or enter final inverse
coverage. No predicted sorting-failure count is an input.

An endpoint with an oriented $3$-cycle clears that cycle in one move by
\cite{framework}, \nolinkurl{cor:oriented-three-cycles}, and closes whenever
$1+\TD(10)\leq 7$. A whole endpoint domain already satisfying
$\TD(13)\leq 7$ is elementary in its entirety.

The complete emitter and checker Burnside censuses agree on all 74
annotations: 76,048,061 classes in total, of which 61,798,462 lie outside
these elementary closures. These are obligation counts, not sorting failures.

\section{Encoding, symmetry, counting, and generation}
For a fixed-point-free annotated type, a fixed-content word assigns a color to
each algebraic cycle. An unoriented color supplies repeated unranked beads;
an oriented color supplies each cyclic rank once, excluding the unoriented
rank order. Positions follow $\beta$. Auxiliary symmetries rename equal
annotated colors and shift oriented rank origins. Auxiliary standardization
chooses colors by first occurrence and sets the first oriented rank to zero.

Position rotation and reflection coupled with oriented-rank inversion form the
twisted dihedral action. A twisted bracelet is an orbit, not a canonical word,
and its reflection is not ordinary word reversal. Decoder equivariance and full
target realizability identify realizable orbits with extended-toric classes of
valid pairs~\cite{framework}. Use \nolinkurl{TwistedBraceletKey} for candidate
orbits and \nolinkurl{ExtendedToricClassKey} only after pair validation.

The Burnside counter counts the endpoint classes; direct generation emits one
representative per class and is checked against those counts. Roots use rank
placement with normalized cycle origins and equal-cycle labels. Forced proper
target cycles and strict smaller twisted images reject prefixes. All
source-side move sequences are derived before enumeration. A known target path
from $a$ to $b$ avoiding $c$ forces cyclic order $(a,b,c)$ in every completion,
permitting additional target-side immediate-closure rejection. Complete remaining
leaves pass an exact two-sided selection test. The independent checker derives
its predicates from arbitrary supports, changes the full cycles directly, and
has no emitter-artifact dependency. Small-domain class-set checks precede large
counts. Counting is separate from sorting.


For clarity, let $\mathcal V$ be the realizable permutation arrays of one
fixed annotated type with the current cycle relabeled to
$(0,1,\ldots,N-1)$. Auxiliary standardization removes only color names and
oriented-rank origins: it does not identify distinct arrays in $\mathcal V$.
The twisted dihedral group $D_N$ acts on these arrays. On words its reflection
maps a ranked bead $(c,r)$ to $(c,-r\bmod\ell_c)$, where $\ell_c$ is
the length of color $c$, while reversing positions;
unranked beads keep their color. Decoder equivariance gives
\[
 \#\text{classes}=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{g\in D_N}
       |\{v\in\mathcal V:g\cdot v=v\}|.
\]
The shared framework proves the orbit correspondence and fixed-set counting
construction~\cite{framework}; the companion Burnside note details the
implemented rotation and reflection terms. Counting candidates before target
realizability would not give this class count.

Here is why the constrained root enumeration covers every residual class.
Write the algebraic cycles on consecutive abstract ranks with the prescribed
lengths, and assign their ranks injectively to positions around the current
cycle. Choose each cycle origin at its least position and order equal-length
cycles by their origins. A position rotation places an origin of a longest
cycle at zero. These choices remove auxiliary multiplicities while preserving
a representative of every class. Compare all allowed anchored rotations and
twisted reflections; retaining a least image leaves one class representative.
A strict partial lexicographic rejection is safe only after the first differing
images are both assigned. A proper target cycle, once forced by assigned edges,
cannot be extended to the required full target cycle and is also safely rejected.

The remaining prefix rejections certify an actual selected route. For an
all-current sequence, applicability depends on the cyclic orders of its three
support symbols after each preceding segment interchange. Once all these ranks
are assigned, the test is independent of later placements. A successful test
therefore excludes only completions already covered by the selection argument.
The target-path rejection above has the same property. Every surviving complete
array is validated for full target realizability and tested against all
remaining two-sided routes. Thus no residual class is omitted. Independent
forward and inverse small-domain oracles check complete class sets rather than
merely their cardinalities; full-domain counts are then independently regenerated.

\subsection{The zero-move allowance and capped all-odd searches}
Here a \emph{zero move} preserves the number of odd-length cycles; this is
distinct from a move of fixed-symbol gain zero. Let
$h=\ell_3(\omega)=(N-c_{\rm odd}(\omega))/2$, including fixed points.
This is the minimum number of unrestricted algebraic $3$-cycles in a
factorization of $\omega$~\cite[Proposition 3.1]{muhle-nadeau}, hence a
lower bound for sorting transpositions. In a complete sorting of length $L$
using only zero moves and moves increasing $c_{\rm odd}$ by two, the number
of zero moves is $L-h$. Thus a budget $B$ permits at most $B-h$ zero moves.
The ordinary rank lower-bound test already enforces this allowance.

Let $D(m)$ denote the known diameter for a contracted domain of $m$ symbols,
with an unknown entry interpreted as $+\infty$. Define
\[
 R=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup
 \{r\geq1:\text{ some }m\text{ satisfies }2r+1\leq m\leq N,
                                      \ D(m)\leq r\}\bigr).
\]
Set $R=0$ when diameter closures are disabled. The rank zero case represents
identity. For the current table and $N\geq3$, $R=1$ when closures are enabled.

\begin{lemma}[Capped all-odd search with no zero-move allowance]
At a search node, let $b$ be its remaining full sorting budget and $k$ its
remaining exploration depth. Suppose every cycle of $\omega$ has odd length
and $h=b$. If $h>k+R$, no explored continuation can reach identity or a
diameter closure within those limits.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
After $t$ permitted moves containing $z$ zero moves, the residual rank is
$r=h-t+z$, while the remaining budget is $h-t$. If $z>0$, the rank lower
bound excludes both completion and a sound diameter closure, including one
whose unsearched suffix uses unrestricted sorting moves. Any potentially
successful prefix therefore consists entirely of rank-decreasing moves.
An all-odd permutation remains all odd under such moves
by~\cite[Corollary 3.3 and Remark 3.5]{muhle-nadeau}.
Contract its fixed points. If $r>0$ and $c$ nontrivial cycles remain, its
domain size is $m=2r+c\geq2r+1$. A diameter closure with remaining budget
$r$ consequently requires $r\leq R$. But $t\leq k$ implies
$r=h-t\geq h-k>R\geq0$, excluding both a closure and identity.
\end{proof}

CUDA derives $R$ from the actual supplied diameter table and applies the
strict inequality only when the rank equals the remaining full budget.
This rejects a subtree as unresolved; it never discharges an obligation or
omits any required inverse reconstruction.

\section{Certificates, persistence, and conditional conclusion}
Every persisted set has a version, deterministic keys, explicit budgets,
domains and counts, and rejects missing data. Large inverse sets are disk-backed;
asynchronous certificate persistence uses bounded queues. A witness proves an
upper bound only for its represented class. During emission, separate CPU and
GPU worker pools communicate through a versioned disk queue. A second queue
transfers every GPU miss to an independent CPU inverse pool. Every source
class is accounted for, every GPU result is classified, and every transferred
inverse case is processed before the next wave is sealed. All three pools
are joined; interrupted queues are regenerated from the sealed source wave.
Inverse outputs use deterministic writer shards with bounded queues. Shards
sort distinct keys in unsigned byte order, build SST files independently,
and ingest them with global sequence numbers enabled. Every repeated key
has the same empty value, so overlapping tables represent one set entry.
Every
partial output batch is queued and every writer is drained and joined before
the exact inventory is checked; a generation or persistence failure prevents
sealing. This changes persistence scheduling without changing the valid-pair
keys or the obligations regenerated by the independent checker.
CUDA searches exclude fixed symbols from move supports, permit an increase of
zero or two in the number of odd-length cycles, and exclude supports in three
distinct algebraic cycles. CPU prefix searches do not apply the last
restriction. Neither exhaustion of this restricted CUDA tree nor a depth limit
or timeout certifies a distance lower bound. All these outcomes preserve the unresolved
obligation; only a replayed witness or diameter closure discharges it.
The CPU checker regenerates every
root obligation, every endpoint class and every inverse wave, and replays
witnesses from the certificate database. Emitter checkpoints are performance
state, not proof inputs. Checkpoint versions identify the exact route and state layout; checksums and sidecar manifests are not proof inputs.

If all active direct roots and final reconstructed roots have checked
budget-10 results and every endpoint is discharged or has complete inverse
coverage, the exhaustive case partition gives $\TD(18)\leq 10$.
Together with the established reverse-permutation lower bound this would give
$\TD(18)=10$. The completion criteria require an independent full-domain
count and full end-to-end verification; partial counts and software tests are
not the conditional conclusion.
